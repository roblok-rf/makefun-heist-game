# MODELS.md — 15 concepts for "Make the best heist game"

ATM method: the bounty opened at 12:59 +07 on 22 Nov 2026 and its leaderboard
was still empty when this build started (checked ~13:07 +07, both the bounty
page and the leaderboard page — no entries listed, no scores) — no live entries
to study yet. The patterns below therefore combine (a) the judge's own five
criteria in the kit's RULES.txt, (b) the house starter (a plain dodge-and-collect
game — the floor every entry must beat), and (c) the winning patterns from the
sibling stealth bounty the same week ("Nightshade: The Silent Gallery":
patrol → `?` → `!` guard states, cones you read before you move, Ghost bonus,
three wings, camera cones in the later wings). Model 11 is the submitted game.

1. **One-room smash-and-grab** — a single room, one guard, grab the loot and
   run. Simple and readable, but no depth: one route, no tools, no tension arc.
2. **The starter, reskinned** — dodge falling blocks rebadged as guards. The
   kit warns the starter handed in unchanged plays as a house game; a reskin
   is the same game with a hat on. Rejected.
3. **Turn-based heist** — move on a grid one step at a time, guards move after
   you. Fair and readable, but slow, and the judge's "tension as cones sweep"
   needs real time. Rejected.
4. **Payday-style loud heist** — drill, alarms, waves of guards, shoot your
   way out. Fun, but it copies a commercial game's shape and loses the stealth
   the criteria reward. Rejected.
5. **Laser-grid puzzle** — timing puzzles through laser beams, no guards. The
   cones are the game's identity; lasers alone make a timing game, not a heist.
6. **Getaway-driver chase** — steal the loot, then a driving escape. Two games
   in one, neither finished; the criteria want one complete heist in one frame.
7. **Crew management sim** — hire a hacker, a driver, a lookout; watch timers.
   A management screen is not a room the thief is crossing. Rejected.
8. **Procedural mansion** — a new random floor plan every run. Replayable, but
   the screenshots would show a generic tile set and the judge reads the cover
   first; hand-designed rooms read better at a glance.
9. **Guard-play reversal** — you play the guard catching the thief. Original,
   but the brief is a heist game: the thief's side is the fantasy. Rejected.
10. **One heist, three approaches** — the same room, three loadouts (ghost /
    loud / gadget). Deep, but one room means one screenshot story; three
    different rooms show more game.
11. **THE MERIDIAN JOB (submitted)** — three hand-designed heists (Gallery /
    Vault Below / Penthouse), guards with yellow cones (patrol → `?` →
    `!` chase), sweeping cameras with red cones, hold-E cracking, an alarm
    meter (55 hunted, 100 busted), coin lures, smoke bombs, loot + target +
    Ghost/Clean/time bonuses, and title / briefing / pause / caught / getaway
    screens. Combines the stealth bounty's proven guard states with the heist
    brief's cracking and getaway, and the judge's first criterion is met by
    the play screenshot: thief, guard cone, camera cone, target, alarm meter
    in one frame.
12. **Heist + hacking minigame** — crack the case with a lockpicking puzzle.
    Adds a second mechanic, but a minigame overlay hides the room at the
    moment of highest tension. The hold-E crack keeps the room visible.
13. **Day/night cycle heist** — the same museum by day (crowds, disguise) and
    by night (guards, darkness). Ambitious, but disguise/crowd systems double
    the scope and the night half is model 11 anyway.
14. **Co-op two-thief heist** — two players, one distracts, one cracks. The
    bounty is played with keyboard and mouse by one person; a second thief
    controlled by the same keys is a gimmick, not a game.
15. **Roguelite heist run** — loot between runs buys permanent upgrades.
    Progression is nice, but the judge compares one screenshot sheet and one
    source outline; a meta layer never shows. Depth belongs in the room, not
    outside it.
